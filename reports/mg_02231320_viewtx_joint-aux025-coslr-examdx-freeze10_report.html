2 — Pipeline Overview

2.1 — Model Architecture

View Encoder (effv2s): Each of the 4 mammogram views (LCC, LMLO, RCC, RMLO) is encoded independently by an EfficientNetV2S backbone. The backbone produces a fixed-length feature vector per image. Weights are shared across views.
View Aggregation (Transformer): The 4 per-view feature vectors are treated as a sequence of tokens with learnable positional embeddings for view identity and laterality. A multi-head self-attention Transformer aggregates across tokens to produce a single exam-level representation capturing cross-view context.
Task Heads: The exam embedding fans out to task-specific linear prediction heads:
  • Auxiliary classification (per-view): view, laterality, target, filter, manufacturer, bc
  • Auxiliary regression (per-view): kvp, mas, age, months_to_dx
  • Risk head (exam-level): discrete hazard rates $h_1 \ldots h_{5}$ for risk (5 yr)

2.2 — Data Flow

B exams × 4 views → view encoder → B×4 feature vectors → reshape to sequences → Transformer → B exam embeddings → task heads.
Aux heads receive the per-view features before pooling. Risk head receives the exam-level pooled embedding. Images are normalised per-image to zero mean / unit variance before encoding.

2.3 — Patient-Level Data Split

No patient leakage: all exams from the same patient are assigned to the same train/val fold. Stratification ensures comparable cancer-positive rates across folds. Multiple exams per patient are treated independently at training time but share fold membership.

2.4 — Staged Curriculum Learning

Phase 1 — Backbone Pretrain (epochs 0–6): Auxiliary and regression tasks only; risk head weight = 0. Goal: initialise the backbone with clinically meaningful low-level features (view geometry, exposure parameters, patient age) before the risk signal is introduced. Prevents the risk head from corrupting the backbone with noisy early gradients.
Phase 2 — Taper (epochs 6–26): Aux weight linearly annealed 1.0→0.1; risk weight ramped 0.0→1.0 over 20 epochs. Smooth transition avoids the instability of abruptly switching objectives.
Phase 3 — Risk Training (epoch 26+): Risk head dominates (weight = 5.0). Auxiliary heads retained at weight 0.1 as regularisation — they prevent the encoder from over-fitting to the risk label distribution and maintain view-level feature quality.

2.5 — Gradient Accumulation & Effective Batch

Micro-batch: 20 exams (80 images/exam → 80 images/step). Accumulated over 4 steps before the optimiser update, giving an effective batch of 80 exams without the GPU memory cost of loading them simultaneously. Learning rate is set relative to the effective batch size.

2.6 — Discrete Hazard Formulation

Per-interval hazard rates $h_t = \sigma(z_t)$ composed into cumulative risk:
$$F(t) = 1 - \prod_{k=1}^{t}(1 - h_k)$$
Cumulative risk within $t$ years
Loss: BCE at each observable interval $t \in \mathcal{M}$: $$\mathcal{L} = -\frac{1}{|\mathcal{M}|} \sum_{t \in \mathcal{M}} \Big[ y_t \log h_t + (1-y_t)\log(1-h_t)\Big]$$
